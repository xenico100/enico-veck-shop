Digital-to-Atelier Architecture

Brand manifesto & innovation workflow

Cloth is the last step,
not the first idea.

ENICO VECK treats a collection as an episode: a script, a set, a cast of garments. The script is written in pattern data. Nothing is cut until the garment already exists in three dimensions.

“I build the garment twice — once as code and geometry, once as cloth. The second build only confirms what the first one proved.”

I design, pattern, prototype, sew, shoot and sell alone. That constraint shaped the method: instead of sampling physically until something works, I resolve proportion, seam placement and fabric behaviour inside CLO 3D, where a change costs minutes rather than metres of cloth.

The digital garment is not a picture of the product — it is the product’s first prototype. It carries the pattern, the marker, the consumption figure and the fit decision, and it is the file the paper pattern is printed from. When the cloth is finally cut, the design questions are already closed, so the atelier time goes into the hand work that a screen cannot fake: pressing, fusing, seam finishing, the weight of a hem.

01

Virtual drafting

Pattern drafted in 2D, assembled as a 3D garment, dressed on an avatar at the target size. Silhouette, collar stand, pocket placement and ease are judged in motion before a single metre is bought.

CLO 3D
.jpj pattern + 3D garment
02

Marker & consumption

The marker is arranged inside the same file, so fabric order, cost and waste are read directly off the pattern before sampling — the decision that usually happens after the first sample is already paid for.

Consumption 75.3%
Wastage 24.7%
03

Atelier production

Cut, fused, pressed and sewn in the Seoul studio on a single industrial straight-stitch machine. With no overlocker in the room, every raw edge is resolved by design: turned and top-stitched, bound, or enclosed with fusible interfacing and a woven lining.

100% in-house
one machine · one pair of hands
04

Publishing

The finished piece is photographed in the same room and published to enicoveck.com, where the archive is a navigable 3D space rather than a grid — the garment’s technical record and its image live together.

Cinema Episode 02 —
Deep Black

A chapter built around one colour and one attitude: black held in leather, denim, cotton and a paper-thin gloss, then reassembled across outerwear and trousers. Ten pieces, front and back, from the archive of the episode.

A long navy body interrupted by a gloss-black cape that behaves like a second skin: funnel collar, storm layer, tonal hardware, a hem that falls to mid-calf. The silhouette is drawn so the two surfaces read as one garment from the front and as two from the side.

Painted Mark

Technical spec & designer profile

One designer,
one line of production.

Hyungseok Baek

Designer and founder of ENICO VECK, Seoul. Trained in fashion design and CLO 3D — sixteen sessions teaching the software alongside his own practice — with earlier work in uniform and sample development. He runs the brand end to end: concept, pattern, 3D prototype, cutting, sewing, pressing, photography and the store itself.

The brand exists because the two halves of fashion — the digital and the physical — are usually separated by a supply chain. Here they happen in one pair of hands, which is why the drawings, the patterns, the garments and the photographs can all be held to the same standard.
